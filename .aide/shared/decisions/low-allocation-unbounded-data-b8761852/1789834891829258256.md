# low-allocation-unbounded-data

**Decision:** Where practical, prefer low- or zero-allocation access patterns for potentially unbounded lists, strings, maps and other growing data sets, especially on repeated read and hot paths. Avoid unnecessary full materialization, copying and decoding proportional to total data size.

## Rationale

Allocation churn and retained memory can limit scalability even when individual operations are fast. Choose access patterns that remain efficient as data volume and request concurrency grow.

## Details

Prefer streaming, iteration, pagination or bounded batches where they fit the operation. Reuse storage only with clear ownership, lifetimes and concurrency safety; avoid retaining oversized backing storage. Consider immutable precomputed views only where measured benefit justifies their synchronization and invalidation lifecycle. Preserve correctness, authorization freshness, Raft apply/restore semantics, resource limits and maintainability. This is a design preference, not an absolute zero-allocation requirement or a mandate to retrofit unrelated code, add pools/caches, or use unsafe techniques. Measure ns/op, B/op and allocs/op across representative sizes before adopting nontrivial optimizations. If the tradeoff is uncertain, present it to the user. Policy allocation follow-up: #360; #309 was closed after benchmarking, with runtime caching deferred.
